This article shows you how to connect your organization's identity provider to WSO2 Cloud, so people in your organization can sign in with their work account instead of Google or GitHub.

## How it works

- **OpenID Connect.** Your identity provider must support OpenID Connect (OIDC).
- **One identity provider per organization.** You can edit it or point it at a different provider, but you can't delete it. To stop using it, turn it off.
- **Sign in with the organization handle.** People choose **Continue with enterprise sign-in** on the sign-in page and enter your [organization handle](./organizations.mdx#how-an-organization-is-identified).
- **New users join the Viewer group.** The first time someone signs in with enterprise sign-in, they join your organization in the **Viewer** group. See [Access control](./access-control.mdx#groups).

## Prerequisites

- Membership in the **Admin** group of the organization. Other members can see whether enterprise sign-in is on, but can't change it.
- Admin access to your identity provider, to register WSO2 Cloud as a client application.

## Register WSO2 Cloud at your identity provider

1. Sign in to the [WSO2 Cloud console](https://console.cloud.wso2.com).
2. In the top bar, select **Settings**.
3. Under **Organization**, select **Enterprise sign-in**.
4. Select **Set up identity provider**.
5. Copy the **Redirect URI**. It's set by WSO2 Cloud and stays the same if you later change provider.
6. At your identity provider, create an OIDC client application with that redirect URI. Note its client ID and client secret.

## Connect your identity provider

On the **Set up identity provider** page, fill in the details of the client you created:

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Client ID** | The client identifier your identity provider issued. |
| **Client secret** | The client secret your identity provider issued. It's stored write-only, and the console never shows it again. |
| **Authorization endpoint** | Your provider's authorization endpoint, such as `https://idp.example.com/authorize`. |
| **Token endpoint** | Your provider's token endpoint, such as `https://idp.example.com/token`. |
| **Issuer** | Your provider's issuer, such as `https://idp.example.com`. |
| **UserInfo endpoint** | Your provider's UserInfo endpoint, such as `https://idp.example.com/userinfo`. |
| **JWKS endpoint** | Your provider's JWKS endpoint, such as `https://idp.example.com/.well-known/jwks.json`. |
| **Scopes** | Optional. Space-separated. Leave it empty to use the default scopes shown in the field. |
| **Prompt** | Optional. A prompt to send to your provider, such as `select_account` or `consent`. |

Your provider publishes its endpoints and issuer in its OIDC discovery document, usually at `https://<provider>/.well-known/openid-configuration`.

Under **Attribute mappings**, you can map the attributes your provider sends to WSO2 Cloud user attributes. Without mappings, attributes keep the names your provider sends.

Select **Create**.

## Turn on enterprise sign-in

1. On the **Enterprise sign-in** page, turn on **Use for sign-in**.
2. Confirm.

The page shows **On**, and the organization handle people enter to sign in.

When **Use for sign-in** is off, nobody can use enterprise sign-in. The configuration is kept, so you can turn it back on.

## Edit the configuration

1. On the **Enterprise sign-in** page, select **Edit configuration**.
2. Change the fields you need. Leave **Client secret** unchanged to keep the stored secret.
3. Select **Save changes**.

If you change the provider, WSO2 Cloud asks you to confirm, and uses the new settings as soon as you save. A new provider may send attributes with different names, so check your attribute mappings.
